## 0. What we are actually selling

Not a QR code. Not an app. Not a blockchain.

We sell a brand the ability to say, to one customer holding one bottle, in
under ten seconds and on a feature phone: **this specific unit is genuine, and
nobody has claimed it before you.**

Everything in this plan exists to make that sentence true at scale, for
companies who are not us, without any of them being able to hurt each other.

The insight v1 proved, and the thing every competitor gets wrong: **the QR is
not the security.** A QR can be photographed off a shelf and printed onto ten
thousand counterfeits, and every scan will show the correct product name. The
security is a hidden, one-time credential under scratch foil, plus a central
record of who has already used it. HAKIKI authenticates *units*, not *codes on
labels*.

That distinction is the product. It is also the hardest thing to explain in a
sales meeting, which is why the pricing page has to lead with the failure mode
of the alternative rather than with our feature list.

### Who buys this

| Segment | Why they bleed | What they will pay for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cosmetics and skincare | Counterfeits cause chemical burns; one viral injury kills a brand | Proof of authenticity a customer can check before use |
| Pharma and supplements | Regulatory exposure, TMDA/TFDA pressure | Auditable serialisation, recall by batch |
| Agri-inputs (seed, agro-chem) | Fake fertiliser destroys a season; farmers are rural and offline | SMS-first verification, no smartphone needed |
| Spirits and beverages | Refilled bottles | Tamper-evident label + one-time credential |
| Automotive and electrical parts | Safety liability, insurance | Serialised warranty tied to the unit |

Start with cosmetics because we have a live reference customer, the pain is
acute and emotional, and the labels are small enough that our 45 x 40 mm
artwork already fits. Expand outward along the same physics.

### Why now, in this market

East African brands are being counterfeited faster than they can respond, and
the existing answers are all wrong for the context. Global track-and-trace
platforms price in USD per unit and assume smartphones. Local "scan the QR"
solutions authenticate the QR, which is security theatre. Nobody has built the
offline path — SMS, feature phone, rural — as a first-class channel rather than
an afterthought. That gap is the whole opportunity.

## 1. Honest assessment of what exists today

v1 is running in production for one company, with 50,000 live units across
three products. It is genuinely good, and it is genuinely not a SaaS.

### What is already production-grade and should be kept

- **The verification engine.** One Postgres function, one transaction, a row
  lock on the unit. Twenty concurrent submissions of one stolen code are
  counted as twenty distinct people. This is the core asset and it is correct.
- **The credential model.** HMAC for lookup, AES-256-GCM for controlled
  reprint, both keys in the environment. A database dump alone mints nothing.
- **The fraud rules.** Deterministic, defensible, explainable to a customer or
  a regulator. No model to retrain, no scores to argue about.
- **The activation gate.** Units are inert until reconciled onto product, so a
  stolen label roll authenticates nothing. This is the feature nobody asks for
  and everybody needs.
- **The SMS discipline.** Success confirmed positively against documented
  gateway codes, never inferred from the absence of an error word. Plain ASCII
  so messages stay one GSM-7 segment.
- **The message copy.** `INVALID_CODE` never says "FAKE", because the customer
  may simply have mistyped and accusing a genuine buyer is its own harm.

### What is single-tenant and must change

| Today | Why it breaks as SaaS |
| --- | --- |
| One global `HAKIKI_HMAC_SECRET` | One leak compromises every customer's codes |
| RLS enabled with **no policies**, everything through the service key | No tenant isolation in the database; one bad query crosses brands |
| One company row, seeded by script | No signup, no provisioning, no self-serve |
| One Mobishastra account, one sender ID | Sender IDs are per-brand and take days to register |
| Label artwork hardcoded to MIKENOVA gold | Every brand needs their own |
| Print files rendered on a laptop | 50,000 units took an afternoon and two failure modes |
| `verify-admin` with one role set | No per-brand users, no distributors, no agencies |
| Codes generated by a Node script | No queue, no idempotency, no progress that survives a laptop lid closing |

### What we learned the hard way, and must design around

These came out of shipping the first 50,000 units. Each is a product
requirement, not a war story.

1. **A specimen sheet and a live sheet are indistinguishable to a human.** The
   pilot "failed" for a week because the only PDF in the folder was the
   specimen one; its codes existed nowhere. *Requirement: every artefact states
   what it is, and the platform never produces an unlabelled one.*

2. **Artwork that misdescribes its own codes is the same bug again.** A sheet
   stamped "these codes verify as genuine" was generated for a batch whose
   units were all still inert. *Requirement: artwork reads live unit state,
   never assumes.*

3. **Chrome writes short PDFs and reports success.** A 21-page layout came out
   as 17, then 14, then 12 — a race between printing and parsing. Caught only
   because we counted pages in the finished file. *Requirement: every generated
   artefact is verified against its expected shape before delivery, and page
   count is checked, not assumed.*

4. **The activation gate is the most expensive mistake available.** Ship
   20,000 bottles before activating and every honest customer is told their
   product was never approved for sale, while the console fills with 20,000
   fraud alerts. *Requirement: activation cannot be forgotten — the platform
   must nag, block, or stage it.*

5. **Product data invented during setup reaches customers.** A seed script
   guessed "250ml" for a 300ml product. That size printed in every SMS receipt.
   *Requirement: onboarding treats catalogue data as customer-verified, with an
   explicit confirmation step, and no placeholder ever becomes live.*

6. **Timing windows quietly become product rules.** A 60-second double-submit
   guard meant a customer checking twice in a row was told "genuine, first
   time" both times — the exact opposite of a one-time credential. *Requirement:
   every threshold is per-tenant configurable and surfaced in the console with
   its consequence stated in plain language.*

7. **Carrier NAT collapses verifier identity.** Behind CGNAT many customers
   share one IP, so IP-as-identity under-counts distinct verifiers and
   over-triggers rate limits. *Requirement: phone identity is strongly
   preferred, IP is the fallback, and rate limits are tuned per market.*

## 2. Architecture

### 2.1 Tenancy model

**Decision: shared database, shared schema, row-level security keyed on
`company_id`, with per-tenant encryption keys.**

Rejected alternatives:

- *Database per tenant.* Clean isolation, but 200 customers means 200
  migrations to run and 200 connection pools. Kills our ability to ship.
- *Schema per tenant.* Same problem, slightly cheaper.
- *Shared everything with no RLS (today's model).* One missing `.eq()` in a
  server route leaks another brand's serial list. Unacceptable once we are not
  the only customer.

`company_id` is already on every business record in v1. That was the right call
and it makes this a policy change rather than a migration.

**What changes:** RLS goes from "enabled with no policies, everything through
the service key" to "enabled with policies that constrain every row to the
caller's tenant". Server routes stop using the service key for tenant-scoped
reads and start using a request-scoped role carrying `company_id` as a claim.
The service key survives only for genuinely cross-tenant work: billing
rollups, platform admin, the verification engine itself.

```
                    ┌──────────────────────────────┐
  customer  ───────▶│  verify surface (public)     │
  (phone/web)       │  /v/<token>  ·  SMS inbound  │
                    └──────────────┬───────────────┘
                                   │ no tenant context — the CODE resolves it
                    ┌──────────────▼───────────────┐
                    │  hakiki_verify()             │  SECURITY DEFINER
                    │  one txn, row lock on unit   │  the only cross-tenant path
                    └──────────────┬───────────────┘
                                   │
  brand staff ─────▶┌──────────────▼───────────────┐
  (console)         │  tenant-scoped API           │  RLS: company_id = claim
                    │  batches · alerts · catalog  │
                    └──────────────┬───────────────┘
                                   │
                    ┌──────────────▼───────────────┐
                    │  job runners                 │  code gen · artwork · export
                    └──────────────────────────────┘
```

The verification surface is deliberately outside tenancy. A customer holding a
bottle does not know which company made it and must not have to. The code
itself resolves the tenant — which is also why `secret_code_hmac` must remain
globally unique across all tenants, and why the keyspace maths in §2.3 is a
platform concern rather than a per-customer one.

### 2.2 Key management — the decision that matters most

Today: one `HAKIKI_HMAC_SECRET`, one `HAKIKI_ENCRYPTION_KEY` for everything.

As a SaaS this is unacceptable. One leak, and every brand's codes can be
minted by anyone. Worse, we could never prove to Brand B that Brand A's
compromise did not touch them.

**Decision: per-tenant data keys, wrapped by a platform master key held in a
KMS.**

```
  KMS master key  (never leaves the KMS)
        │ wraps
        ▼
  tenant_keys table:  company_id → wrapped_hmac_key, wrapped_aes_key, version
        │ unwrapped per request, cached in memory with a short TTL
        ▼
  hmac(code, tenant_hmac_key)   ·   aes_gcm(code, tenant_aes_key)
```

Consequences to design for now, not later:

- **Lookup by code alone.** SMS gives us a code and no tenant. We cannot HMAC
  it under 200 different keys per lookup. Fix: the printed code carries a short
  **tenant prefix** — a 2-character alphanumeric issued at signup — so
  `A7 4827 1936 52` resolves the tenant before the HMAC. The prefix is not a
  secret and adds two keypresses. Decide this **before** the first SaaS customer
  prints anything, because it is unchangeable afterwards.
- **Key rotation** is per-tenant and versioned. Rotating re-wraps the data key;
  it does not re-hash existing codes, which is why `key_version` lives on the
  unit row.
- **Offboarding.** Deleting a tenant's wrapped key cryptographically retires
  every code they ever issued. That is the clean delete story, and it is worth
  saying out loud in a security review.
- **Phone pepper stays platform-wide and separate**, so phone identity is
  comparable across tenants for cross-brand fraud rings, while code hashes are
  not. This is a deliberate asymmetry and should be stated in the privacy
  policy.

### 2.3 Keyspace and code length as a platform concern

v1's maths: an invented code verifies as real at `issued / keyspace`. At 10
digits, 9 x 10^9 keyspace, 50,000 units → about 1 in 180,000.

Across a platform this changes. If 200 tenants each issue 50,000 units into a
**shared** code space, the platform has 10 million live codes and a blind guess
hits 1 in 900. That is a platform-level vulnerability created by success.

The tenant prefix from §2.2 solves this too: it partitions the keyspace per
tenant, so each brand's risk depends only on their own issuance. **This is the
strongest argument for the prefix and why it is not optional.**

Console must show, live, while an operator picks a length:

```
  10 digits · 20,000 units  →  1 in 450,000   ACCEPTABLE
  12 digits · 20,000 units  →  1 in 45,000,000  STRONG
```

Default 10. Refuse to save below the ACCEPTABLE threshold without an explicit
typed override that is written to the audit log with the operator's name.

### 2.4 Data model changes

Additive where possible. v1's schema survives largely intact.

```sql
-- new
tenants           -- billing identity, plan, status, code_prefix, region
tenant_keys       -- wrapped data keys, version, rotated_at
tenant_users      -- replaces admin_users; role per tenant, a user may hold several
tenant_domains    -- custom verify domain, cert status
tenant_branding   -- logo, palette, label template, support phone, SMS sender id
sms_providers     -- per-tenant gateway config, per-market routing
usage_events      -- append-only: units_generated, verifications, sms_sent — billing truth
jobs              -- code generation, artwork render, export; status + progress + idempotency key
print_files       -- artefact registry: kind, sha256, expected_pages, verified_at, expires_at

-- changed
companies         -- becomes a child of tenants (a tenant may own several brands)
auth_units        -- + key_version
batches           -- + template_id, + activation_policy
verification_events -- + purchase_place (already shipped in 0007)
```

`usage_events` being append-only and separate from operational tables is
deliberate: billing disputes are resolved by replaying an immutable log, never
by trusting a counter that some migration might have touched.

### 2.5 The print pipeline becomes a service

This is where v1 is furthest from a product, and where today's failures came
from. A 20,000-unit run is 40 PDFs and 840 A4 pages. On a laptop that is fifteen
minutes, two Chrome crashes, and one silently truncated file.

**Decision: artwork rendering is a queued, idempotent job with verified output.**

```
  POST /batches/:id/artwork
        │  enqueue render job, return job id
        ▼
  worker (containerised, headless Chrome, 5 GB bundle limit is fine)
        │  for each 500-label slice:
        │    render → COUNT PAGES IN THE OUTPUT → retry on shortfall
        │    sha256 → upload to private blob → register in print_files
        ▼
  brand downloads via short-lived signed URL, every download audited
```

Non-negotiable properties, each bought with real pain:

- **Verify the artefact, not the intention.** Count pages in the produced PDF
  and compare to expected. Chrome reports success on truncated output.
- **Wait for layout, never a fixed sleep.** Print only when the DOM holds the
  expected page count. A fixed delay is a race and loses at scale.
- **Resume by verified state.** "The file exists" is worthless when the failure
  mode is a valid file with pages missing. Resume on verified page count.
- **Recycle the renderer.** Long-lived browsers accumulate and die mid-run.
- **The CSV is the deliverable.** A variable-data press merges from the CSV;
  the PDF is artwork and proof. Both ship, the CSV is operative.
- **Artefacts expire.** Signed URLs measured in hours. Files purged on a
  schedule. A print file is the crown jewels in plaintext and must not
  accumulate in object storage forever.

### 2.6 Label artwork as templates

v1 hardcodes MIKENOVA gold and the HAKIKI wordmark. Brands need their own, and
some will want the HAKIKI mark visible (trust transfer), others will not
(white-label).

Template system with **locked regions** — the parts a brand may not restyle,
because they are load-bearing:

| Locked | Why |
| --- | --- |
| QR ≥ 20 mm box, 0.54 mm module, EC M, quiet zone inside the artwork | Below ~0.5 mm a module stops surviving ink spread on coated stock |
| QR in 100% K | A rich-black CMYK build mis-registers and fattens modules |
| Scratch instruction **above** the panel, never inside | Silver ink covers the dashed box; text inside it disappears |
| Code inside the panel and nowhere else | The credential must be hidden until purchase |
| Support number present | A warning with no number to call is cruelty |

Brands control: colours outside the QR, logo, wordmark visibility, language
pair, label dimensions above the minimum, material.

The template renders to PDF **and** to a press-ready spec sheet — materials,
scratch ink over release varnish, QR tolerances, what to send back as samples.
v1's pilot sheet had this; the production generator dropped it. It goes back in
as part of the template, because "the printer did not know they had to apply
scratch ink" is an extinction-level failure for a customer.

### 2.7 SMS at multi-tenant scale

Hardest operational problem in the whole plan, and the most underestimated.

- **Sender IDs are per-brand and take days to register**, with the gateway and
  often the regulator. This must start on day one of onboarding, in parallel
  with everything else, or it blocks go-live. Build it into the onboarding
  checklist as a long-lead item with its own status.
- **Gateways enforce IP allowlists.** v1 already runs a relay on a fixed IP
  because Vercel has no static egress. Productised: a small fleet of relays
  per region, each accepting only `{to, text}` so it cannot become an open
  proxy, gateway credentials never leaving the relay.
- **Provider abstraction is already right in v1** — swap Mobishastra for Beem
  or Africa's Talking without touching the engine. Extend to per-tenant,
  per-market routing.
- **Credit exhaustion is silent.** "No More Credits" is a documented response
  containing no error-like word. On-screen verification keeps succeeding while
  every receipt fails. Platform must monitor balance per tenant and alert
  before zero, not after.
- **Cost is the dominant variable** in unit economics. See §4.

### 2.8 The verify surface

- **Custom domains per tenant** (`verify.brand.co.tz`) with automatic certs.
  Trust is the product; a customer should not be redirected to a vendor domain.
- **Short URL path.** A unit URL is currently 51 characters, which forces a
  33-module QR. `/v/<token>` on a short domain drops it to 29 modules — a 14%
  larger module at the same physical size, which is free scanning reliability.
- **Offline-first.** The page must work on a 2G connection and a £30 Android.
  No heavy JS, server-rendered, aggressive caching of everything except the
  verdict.
- **Language per tenant and per market.** Swahili/English today; French,
  Portuguese, Amharic as we expand.

## 3. Implementation phases

Each phase ships something a customer can use. No phase is a refactor with
nothing at the end of it.

### Phase 0 — Harden v1 (4 weeks)

Nothing new. Close the gaps that would embarrass us in a security review, while
Goddess Glow is still the only tenant and mistakes are cheap.

- RLS policies on every table, service key reserved for cross-tenant work
- Migrate `admin_users` → `tenant_users`, introduce `tenants`
- Per-tenant key wrapping behind KMS; migrate Goddess Glow to its own key
- **Decide and implement the tenant code prefix** — unchangeable later
- Move code generation and artwork rendering to a job queue with idempotency
- Artefact verification (page counts, sha256, registry) in the pipeline
- Usage events emitted for every billable action

*Exit criterion:* Goddess Glow runs unchanged on multi-tenant foundations, and
a second tenant can be created by SQL without touching code.

### Phase 1 — Second tenant, manually onboarded (6 weeks)

Prove the model with a real second customer while we still do onboarding by
hand. Deliberately not self-serve — we learn what onboarding actually is by
doing it ourselves, twice.

- Tenant provisioning runbook, executed by us
- Branding and label template system with locked regions
- Per-tenant SMS provider config, sender ID registration tracked as a task
- Console: tenant switching, per-tenant roles
- Billing meter running but invoiced manually

*Exit criterion:* two tenants in production, neither able to see the other's
data, proven by an automated isolation test in CI.

### Phase 2 — Self-serve onboarding (8 weeks)

- Signup, company verification, plan selection
- Guided setup: catalogue → branding → SMS → first batch
- **Catalogue data confirmation step** — nothing placeholder reaches production;
  an explicit "this is correct" per product, recorded with who confirmed it
- Sandbox mode: full system, codes clearly marked as test, artwork stamped
  SPECIMEN, never verifiable as genuine
- Stripe billing on metered usage
- Self-serve print file download with signed URLs and audit

*Exit criterion:* a brand signs up, onboards, and generates a verified test
batch without us touching anything.

### Phase 3 — Scale and operations (8 weeks)

- Regional SMS relay fleet, per-market routing
- Render workers autoscaled; 100,000-unit batches routine
- Custom domains with automatic certs
- Brand analytics: verification heatmap, counterfeit geography from
  `purchase_place`, batch health
- Alert workflow: assign, investigate, resolve, export for law enforcement
- Distributor and retailer roles — scan-to-receive, chain of custody

*Exit criterion:* a 100,000-unit run from batch creation to verified print
files in under an hour, unattended.

### Phase 4 — Ecosystem (ongoing)

- Public API + webhooks for ERP and factory line integration
- Line-side print-and-apply integration (codes issued at the machine)
- NFC and tamper-evident seal support for premium SKUs
- Consumer app: scan history, report a counterfeit, brand-funded rewards
- Regulator portal — read-only batch and recall visibility for TMDA/TBS

## 4. Unit economics

The thing most plans skip and every board asks about first.

### Cost per 10,000 units

| Item | Cost | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Code generation, storage | negligible | Postgres rows |
| Artwork render | ~$0.50 | minutes of container CPU |
| Blob storage, 30-day retention | ~$0.10 | files expire |
| Verification requests | ~$1 | assume 30% of units checked |
| **SMS receipts** | **$25–60** | ~1,500 messages at TZS 20–30 |
| Support, amortised | $10–30 | the real cost is humans |

**SMS dominates.** Every pricing decision follows from that.

### Pricing

**Platform fee + per-unit + SMS pass-through.**

| Plan | Monthly | Included units | Per extra unit | SMS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot | $99 | 10,000 | $0.008 | at cost + 20% |
| Growth | $399 | 100,000 | $0.005 | at cost + 15% |
| Scale | $1,200 | 500,000 | $0.003 | at cost + 10% |
| Enterprise | negotiated | — | — | own gateway |

Per-unit rather than per-verification, because the brand must budget before
printing and cannot forecast verification rate. Verification is the value but
units are the invoice.

SMS passed through transparently with a stated margin. Hiding it invites a
dispute the first month a campaign spikes.

Anchor for the sales conversation: at Growth, 100,000 units costs about
$0.004 each — roughly **one two-hundredth of the label it prints on**, against
a counterfeit problem that can cost a cosmetics brand its licence.

### What makes this a good business

- Gross margin above 80% once SMS is passed through
- Usage grows with the customer's production, not their headcount
- Switching cost is extreme: codes in market are tied to our keys, and a brand
  cannot migrate mid-batch without reprinting
- The data compounds — counterfeit geography across tenants is a defensible
  asset no single brand can build

### What makes it hard

- Deeply operational. Print, ink, gateways, regulators. Not a pure software sale.
- Long sales cycles — brands move at the speed of their packaging redesign
- SMS margins compress as volume grows and brands bring their own gateways
- One security incident ends the company; trust is the entire product

## 5. Security and compliance

### Threat model

| Threat | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Print file leaked at the printer | Signed URLs with hours-long expiry, per-download audit, artefacts purged on schedule, batch stays inert until activation |
| Stolen label roll | Activation gate — unreconciled units answer NOT_ACTIVATED and raise a HIGH alert |
| Database dump | Codes are HMAC + AES only; keys in KMS, never in Postgres |
| Brute-force guessing | Per-tenant keyspace via prefix, rate limits per IP and per phone, brute-force alert after 3 wrong codes against one QR |
| Insider at the platform | Per-tenant keys, service key restricted, every privileged read audited |
| Cross-tenant leak | RLS policies + automated isolation test in CI, run on every deploy |
| Counterfeiter pre-checks codes | Second verification warns regardless of who made it; code is one-time |

### The activation gate deserves its own paragraph

It is the single most valuable feature and the single most dangerous one. Get
it right and a stolen roll is worthless. Get it wrong — ship before activating —
and every honest customer is told their genuine product was never approved for
sale, while the console fills with fraud alerts for real buyers.

The platform must make the mistake hard: activation staged by quantity or by
scanned serials, a blocking warning if a batch is marked shipped while inert,
and a dashboard that shows inert-but-shipped as a red state rather than a
statistic.

### Privacy

- Phone numbers stored only as HMAC plus last four digits
- `purchase_place` is public free text, truncated server-side, rendered as text
  and never as markup
- Data residency per tenant as we expand beyond Tanzania
- Retention policy on verification events, default 24 months

## 6. What we deliberately do not build

- **Blockchain.** Postgres proves whether a credential exists and records its
  history perfectly well. Anyone who asks for this is buying a narrative, not a
  solution, and we should say so.
- **AI fraud scoring**, until deterministic rules demonstrably fail. An alert
  an investigator cannot read back and defend is worthless in a legal dispute.
- **A consumer app as the primary channel.** The customer has a feature phone
  and no data. SMS and a light web page are the product; the app is a Phase 4
  loyalty play.
- **Our own SMS infrastructure.** Aggregators exist and regulatory exposure is
  enormous.
- **Print brokerage.** We certify printers and hand over files. Taking on
  printing means taking on the worst margins and the worst liability.

## 9. The first ninety days

1. **Week 1–2.** Decide the tenant code prefix format. Write the isolation test
   that will run in CI forever. Stand up KMS.
2. **Week 3–6.** Phase 0. Goddess Glow migrated to per-tenant keys with zero
   customer-visible change.
3. **Week 7–8.** Sign a second customer — different vertical, ideally
   agri-inputs, because it stresses SMS and rural offline hardest.
4. **Week 9–14.** Phase 1 with that customer in the room. Onboard them by hand
   and write down every single thing we do.
5. **Week 15.** That written-down list is the Phase 2 specification.

The discipline that matters: **do not build self-serve onboarding before
onboarding two customers by hand.** Everything we would guess about that flow
would be wrong, and the cost of finding out later is a rewrite.

## 10. The one-paragraph version

HAKIKI authenticates individual physical units, not QR codes, using a hidden
one-time credential and a central record of who has already claimed it. v1 runs
in production with 50,000 units across three products. Turning it into a SaaS
is four phases of work — tenant isolation with per-tenant keys, a queued and
self-verifying print pipeline, self-serve onboarding, and regional SMS
operations — with the tenant code prefix being the one decision that cannot be
deferred. The economics work because SMS is passed through and everything else
is close to free, the moat is the offline path and the accumulating counterfeit
data, and the whole thing dies if we ever leak a print file. Build it in that
order, and do not automate onboarding until we have done it by hand twice.
